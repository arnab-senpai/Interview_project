An interviewer says: "Design a view counter for a news site. Every article shows how many times it has been read." Then they go quiet and wait.

Most people start drawing boxes. Ten minutes later they are deep in which database to use, and they still don't know how many views a second the counter has to absorb, whether the number must be exact, or how stale it may be. The interviewer has learned little about how they think, and the design rests on guesses.

This chapter gives you one way to spend the time that works for any design question: agree on what to build, put numbers on it, draw the simplest design that works, then let the numbers point at the part to improve. It walks through the view counter as the example, so every later chapter can follow the same steps.
